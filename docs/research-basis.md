# Research basis for ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md

Compiled 2026-11-24. Every arXiv ID below was resolved against its `arxiv.org/abs/<id>` metadata (title and authors checked); peer-reviewed venues are noted where confirmed. This is a curated, verifiable corpus of work that directly concerns agentic adversarial review and the mechanisms it depends on — multi-agent disagreement, evidence grounding, independent review, arbitration, eval-grounded benchmarks, and the failure modes of judge agents. It is not a claim of literal completeness of everything ever posted to arXiv; new work can be added through the repository's issue process.

**Why this list exists.** The standard makes empirical-sounding claims: that self-review shares blind spots, that independent challenge filters weak findings, that agreement is not evidence, that severity needs calibration, and that re-review after fixes matters. Those claims should be traceable to evidence, including evidence that cuts against them. Each entry is tagged with the spec feature it bears on:

- **FC** false consensus / agreement without evidence
- **EG** evidence grounding (findings must cite the artifact)
- **CI** independent or cross-model review
- **SC** structured contract / protocol design
- **HA** human arbiter / human-in-the-loop
- **ST** severity taxonomy
- **TC** token-cost trade-off
- **FR** fix-forward re-review
- **PL** per-lens or per-dimension verdicts
- **BI** judging bias and arbitration

## 1. Direct agentic adversarial review

- **arXiv:2608.18167** — Eric S. Qiu & Joyce Gill (2026), *Adversarial Review: Structured Disagreement for Grounded Agentic Code Review*. Accepted ICML 2026 Workshop on DL4C. Reviewer R + critic C over a frozen artifact; AR reaches 87% on LiveCodeBench vs 82% for five-agent MARS; naive AR scores F1 = 0.457 on SWE-PRBench, and typed disagreement (`DISAGREE_EVIDENCE` / `DISAGREE_CONCERN`) raises it to 0.533. Names and characterizes **false consensus** and **scope creep**. **FC, EG, SC, FR, TC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18167
- **arXiv:2604.19049** — Abhinav Agarwal (2026), *Refute-or-Promote: An Adversarial Stage-Gated Multi-Agent Review Methodology for High-Precision LLM-Assisted Defect Discovery*. Adversarial kill mandates, cold-start reviewers to avoid anchoring, context asymmetry, and a Cross-Model Critic; documents unanimous same-family agreement on wrong findings. **FC, EG, CI, SC, ST**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19049
- **arXiv:2611.23283** — Jiawei Li, Hoa Khanh Dam & Iftekhar Ahmed (2026), *When Single Agent Is Enough: Rethinking Multi-Agent Systems for Automated Code Review*. Systematic comparison of single- and multi-agent designs for review-comment generation and code refinement; multi-agent complexity is not always justified. **Challenges blanket multi-agent adoption; TC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23283
- **arXiv:2603.15911** — Suzhen Zhong, Shayan Noei, Ying Zou & Bram Adams (2026), *Human-AI Synergy in Agentic Code Review*. 278,790 review conversations: AI-agent suggestions are adopted less often than human ones and are frequently incorrect; calls for better project context and multi-turn responsiveness. **HA, EG**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15911
- **arXiv:2606.13175** — Martin Monperrus (2026), *The End of Code Review: Coding Agents Supersede Human Inspection*. Position paper arguing that agent review can replace human inspection; useful as the strongest counterpoint to our human-arbiter and governance clauses. **Challenges HA**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13175

## 2. Multi-agent debate and structured disagreement

- **arXiv:2305.14325** — Yilun Du, Shuang Li, Antonio Torralba, Joshua B. Tenenbaum & Igor Mordatch (2023; ICML 2024), *Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate*. Foundational "society of minds" result: agents propose and critique each other's reasoning over rounds. **EG, SC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325
- **arXiv:2311.17371** — A. P. Smit et al. (2023; ICML 2024, PMLR v235), *Should we be going MAD? A Look at Multi-Agent Debate Strategies for LLMs*. Debate does not reliably beat self-consistency and ensembling; performance is highly sensitive to hyperparameters and agreement levels. **TC; challenges naive debate**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371
- **arXiv:2305.19118** — Tian Liang et al. (2023; EMNLP 2024), *Encouraging Divergent Thinking in Large Language Models through Multi-Agent Debate*. Identifies Degeneration-of-Thought: self-reflection locks in a stance once the model is confident, even if initially wrong; uses tit-for-tat agents plus a judge. Also flags that LLM judges may be unfair when agents differ. **FC, SC, BI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19118
- **arXiv:2508.17536** — Hyeong Kyu Choi, Xiaojin Zhu & Sharon Li (2025), *Debate or Vote: Which Yields Better Decisions in Multi-Agent Large Language Models?* Majority voting explains most MAD gains; debate alone is a martingale over belief trajectories and does not raise expected correctness without correction-biased updates. **FC, BI, TC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17536
- **arXiv:2511.07784** — Haolun Wu, Zhenkun Li & Lingyao Li (2025), *Can LLM Agents Really Debate? A Controlled Study of Multi-Agent Debate in Logical Reasoning*. Majority pressure suppresses independent correction; reasoning strength and diversity dominate structural parameters such as order and confidence visibility. **FC, CI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07784
- **arXiv:2507.03928** — Yiliu Sun, Zicheng Zhao, Sheng Wan & Chen Gong (2025; Findings ACL 2025), *CortexDebate: Debating Sparsely and Equally for Multi-Agent Debate*. Sparse debate graphs reduce context bloat; equal participation counters overconfident dominance. **TC, FC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03928
- **arXiv:2608.03648** — Hao Wu, Shoucheng Song, Chang Yao & Huaiyu Wan (2026), *Group Perspective Matters: Regulating Debate Relationships Can Mitigate Blind Conformity in Multi-Agent Debate*. Directly targets blind conformity by regulating which agents reference which others. **FC, CI, SC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03648
- **arXiv:2509.11035** — Yu Cui, Hang Fu, Haibin Zhang & Licheng Wang (2025; Findings ACL 2026), *Free-MAD: Consensus-Free Multi-Agent Debate*. Rejects forced consensus and majority voting, citing token overhead and conformity as core failure modes. **FC, TC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11035
- **arXiv:2601.05746** — Zhenghao Li, Zhi Zheng, Wei Chen & Jielun Zhao (2026), *DynaDebate: Breaking Homogeneity in Multi-Agent Debate with Dynamic Path Generation*. Homogeneous agents share reasoning paths and errors; dynamic paths manufacture genuine divergence. **FC, CI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05746
- **arXiv:2607.26212** — Quim Motger, Marc Oriol, Jordi Marco & Xavier Franch (2026), *Multi-Agent Debate Strategies: Survey, Taxonomy, and Challenges*. Taxonomy of 141 MAD studies across participants, interaction mechanisms, and structure — a design-space map for review protocols. **SC, TC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26212
- **arXiv:2603.01221** — Dan Qiao, Binbin Chen, Fengyu Cai & Jianlong Chen (2026), *Epistemic Gain, Aleatoric Cost: Uncertainty Decomposition in Multi-Agent Debate for Math Reasoning*. Bayesian decomposition into epistemic gain and aleatoric cost, with homogeneous vs heterogeneous agent comparisons. **TC, CI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01221
- **arXiv:2408.00989** — Jen-tse Huang, Jiaxu Zhou, Tailin Jin & Xuhui Zhou (2024), *On the Resilience of LLM-Based Multi-Agent Collaboration with Faulty Agents*. Shows how topologies behave when an agent is clumsy or malicious — relevant to a critic that must survive a bad reviewer. **CI, FC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00989

## 3. Self-refinement and the limits of self-correction

- **arXiv:2303.17651** — Aman Madaan et al. (2023; NeurIPS 2023), *Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback*. Same model generates, critiques, and refines; a basis for fix-forward re-review, but without independence it shares the author's blind spots. **FR; challenges CI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651
- **arXiv:2310.01798** — Jie Huang, Xinyun Chen, Swaroop Mishra, Huaixiu Steven Zheng, Adams Wei Yu & Xinying Song (2023; ICLR 2024), *Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet*. Intrinsic self-correction without external feedback often degrades performance — the strongest argument against rubber-stamping one's own diff. **Challenges solo review; supports EG, CI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798

## 4. Multi-agent review and code-generation systems

- **arXiv:2402.02172** — Xunzhu Tang, Kisub Kim, Yewei Song, Cedric Lothritz, Bei Li, Saad Ezzini et al. (2024), *CodeAgent: Autonomous Communicative Agents for Code Review*. Communicative agents plus a QA-Checker that keeps contributions aligned with the original review question. **SC, PL**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02172
- **arXiv:2509.20502** — Xiao Wang, Jia Wang, Yijie Wang, Pengtao Dang et al. (2025), *MARS: Toward More Efficient Multi-Agent Collaboration for LLM Reasoning*. Author, independent reviewers with decision/confidence/justification/errors fields, and a meta-reviewer that consolidates and guides revision. The structured fields and meta-review stage behind AR's five-agent baseline. **PL, SC, FR, TC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20502
- **arXiv:2405.11403** — Md. Ashraful Islam, Mohammed Eunus Ali & Md Rizwan Parvez (2024; ACL 2024), *MapCoder: Multi-Agent Code Generation for Competitive Problem Solving*. Retrieval → planning → code → debugging pipeline; a structural analogue of staged review plus repair. **FR, SC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11403
- **arXiv:2312.13010** — Dong Huang, Jie M. Luck, Michael Luck, Qingwen Bu et al. (2023), *AgentCoder: Multi-Agent-based Code Generation with Iterative Testing and Optimisation*. Separate tester and optimizer agents supply iterative external feedback rather than self-review. **EG, FR, CI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13010
- **arXiv:2502.05664** — Md. Ashraful Islam, Mohammed Eunus Ali & Md Rizwan Parvez (2025; Findings NAACL 2025), *CODESIM: Multi-Agent Code Generation and Problem Solving through Simulation-Driven Planning and Debugging*. Simulation-driven verification of plans and internal debugging before finalizing code. **EG, FR**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05664
- **arXiv:2501.07811** — Ruwei Pan, Hongyu Zhang & Chao Liu (2025), *CodeCoR: An LLM-Based Self-Reflective Multi-Agent Framework for Code Generation*. Checks and prunes intermediate outputs across a multi-agent workflow — analogous to per-stage verdict gates. **PL, SC, FR**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07811

## 5. LLM-as-judge bias and arbitration

- **arXiv:2410.21819** — Koki Wataoka, Tsubasa Takahashi & Ryokan Ri (2024), *Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge*. Judges favor low-perplexity, familiar text even when it is not self-generated — a concrete reason to separate reviewer, challenger, and arbiter. **BI, CI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819
- **arXiv:2406.07791** — Lin Shi, Chiyu Ma, Wenhua Liang, Xingjian Diao, Weicheng Ma & Soroush Vosoughi (2024; AACL-IJCNLP 2025), *Judging the Judges: A Systematic Study of Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge*. Over 150,000 evaluations; position bias is systematic and interacts with judge, task, and quality gap. **BI, SC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791
- **arXiv:2505.19477** — Chiyu Ma, Enpei Zhang, Yilun Zhao et al. (2025; Findings EMNLP 2025), *Judging with Many Minds: Do More Perspectives Mean Less Prejudice? On Bias Amplifications and Resistance in Multi-Agent Based LLM-as-Judge*. Debate-based judging amplifies position, verbosity, CoT, and bandwagon bias after the first round; meta-judging is more resistant. **FC, BI, CI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19477
- **arXiv:2411.16594** — Dawei Li, Bohan Jiang, Liangjie Huang, Alimohammad Beigi, Chengshuai Zhao, Zhen Tan et al. (2024), *From Generation to Judgment: Opportunities and Challenges of LLM-as-a-judge*. Survey and taxonomy of judging methods and benchmark design — background for arbiter design. **BI, SC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16594

## 6. Benchmarks for evidence-grounded claims

- **arXiv:2310.06770** — Carlos E. Jimenez, John Yang, Alexander Wettig, Shunyu Yao, Kexin Pei, Ofir Press et al. (2023; ICLR 2024), *SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues?* Repository-level issue resolution with executable ground truth; used by AR. **EG**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770
- **arXiv:2403.07974** — Naman Jain, King Han, Alex Gu, Wen-Ding Li, Fanjia Yan, Tianjun Zhang et al. (2024), *LiveCodeBench: Holistic and Contamination Free Evaluation of Large Language Models for Code*. Contamination-resistant coding benchmark used by AR. **EG**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974
- **arXiv:2603.26130** — Deepak Kumar (2026), *SWE-PRBench: Benchmarking AI Code Review Quality Against Pull Request Feedback*. Human-annotated PR review ground truth with an LLM judge validated at κ = 0.75; frontier models detect only 15–31% of human-flagged issues. The benchmark where AR's false-consensus failure appeared. **EG, ST, BI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26130
- **arXiv:2603.23448** — Yuntong Zhang, Zhiyuan Pan, Imam Nur Bani Yusuf, Haifeng Ruan et al. (2026), *Code Review Agent Benchmark* (c-CRAB). Benchmark for agents performing PR code review. **EG, ST**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23448
- **arXiv:2608.21311** — Niruthiha Selvanayagam & Taher A. Ghaleb (2026; ESEM 2026), *AI-to-AI Code Reviews of GitHub Pull Requests*. Study of 248,641 AI-authored PRs and AI-attributed reviews; evidence about closed-loop AI review quality. **HA, FC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21311

## 7. Adversarial inputs and review outside code

- **arXiv:2306.05499** — Yi Liu, Gelei Deng, Yuekang Li, Kailong Wang et al. (2023), *Prompt Injection attack against LLM-integrated Applications*. Foundational indirect-injection threat: untrusted repository content can steer an agent that reads it. **SC, EG**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05499
- **arXiv:2406.13352** — Edoardo Debenedetti, Jie Zhang, Mislav Balunović, Luca Beurer-Kellner et al. (2024; NeurIPS 2024), *AgentDojo: A Dynamic Environment to Evaluate Prompt Injection Attacks and Defenses for LLM Agents*. Tool-using agents under adversarial data — directly relevant to review agents reading diffs, issues, and comments. **EG, SC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352
- **arXiv:2503.00061** — Qiusi Zhan, Richard Fang, Henil Shalin Panchal, Daniel Kang et al. (2025; Findings NAACL 2025), *Adaptive Attacks Break Defenses Against Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks on LLM Agents*. Eight IPI defenses bypassed at >50% success — an argument for structural trust boundaries, not prompt-only defenses. **Challenges text-constraint-only fixes**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00061
- **arXiv:2504.19793** — Jiawen Shi, Zenghui Yuan, Guiyao Tie, Pan Zhou et al. (2025; NDSS 2026), *Prompt Injection Attack to Tool Selection in LLM Agents*. Malicious tool documentation hijacks tool choice; a risk when review agents may invoke tools described in repository content. **SC, EG**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19793
- **arXiv:2412.10415** — Nicolas Bougie & Narimasa Watanabe (2024), *Generative Adversarial Reviews: When LLMs Become the Critic*. LLM agents with memory and reviewer personas adversarially critique scientific work — adversarial review outside code. **PL, SC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10415
- **arXiv:2406.12708** — Yiqiao Jin, Qinlin Zhao, Yiyang Wang, Hao Chen et al. (2024; EMNLP 2024), *AgentReview: Exploring Peer Review Dynamics with LLM Agents*. Simulates review, rebuttal, and meta-review dynamics and surfaces reviewer biases. **PL, HA, BI**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12708
- **arXiv:2503.08506** — Xian Gao, Jiacheng Ruan, Zongyun Zhang, Jingsheng Gao et al. (2025), *ReviewAgents: Bridging the Gap Between Human and AI-Generated Paper Reviews*. Reviewer plus meta-review/area-chair agents for reasoning-consistent, human-aligned reviews. **PL, SC, HA**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08506
- **arXiv:2604.26506** — Yuan Xin, Yixuan Weng, Minjun Zhu, Ying Ling et al. (2026), *SafeReview: Defending LLM-based Review Systems Against Adversarial Hidden Prompts*. Co-evolutionary generator/defender training against hidden prompts embedded in submissions. **SC, EG**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26506
- **arXiv:2506.11113** — Tzu-Ling Lin, Wei-Chih Chen, Teng-Fang Hsiao, Hou-I Liu, Ya-Hsin Yeh et al. (2025; Findings EMNLP 2025), *Breaking the Reviewer: Assessing the Vulnerability of Large Language Models in Automated Peer Review Under Textual Adversarial Attacks*. Textual perturbations distort review scores — support for mandatory evidence-grounding. **EG**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11113
- **arXiv:2604.27924** — Sihong Wu, Owen Jiang, Yilun Zhao, Tiansheng Hu et al. (2026), *Can AI Be a Good Peer Reviewer? A Survey of Peer Review Process, Evaluation, and the Future*. Survey of review generation, rebuttals, meta-reviews, and decisions — maps onto a review → re-review protocol. **PL, FR, SC**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27924

## Corrections to third-party sources

- The [Heym blog](https://heym.run/blog/ai-code-review) cites "Du et al., arXiv" with ID **2305.19118**, but that ID is Liang et al., *Encouraging Divergent Thinking in Large Language Models through Multi-Agent Debate*. Du et al.'s multi-agent debate paper is **arXiv:2305.14325**. Both are cited correctly above.
- CodeSim is **arXiv:2502.05664**, MapCoder is **arXiv:2405.11403**, and MARS is **arXiv:2509.20502** (a general multi-agent review framework, not code-review-specific).
- The blog's other references (IBM, GitHub, TechCrunch, Cloudflare, Microsoft, Veracode, Faros AI) are industry sources, not academic papers, and are intentionally absent from this bibliography.
